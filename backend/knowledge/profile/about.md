## Engineering Philosophy

### Understand the Problem Before Writing Code

I believe good engineering starts with understanding the problem rather than immediately jumping into implementation. Before choosing a solution, I aim to understand the requirements, constraints, expected behavior, and edge cases. A clear understanding of the problem helps me avoid unnecessary complexity and build solutions that address the actual need.

### Understand the Root Cause

When something breaks or behaves unexpectedly, I prefer investigating the underlying cause instead of relying on quick fixes. I aim to reproduce the problem, examine relevant logs and code, form hypotheses, and test possible explanations. I see debugging as an opportunity to understand the system more deeply.

### Choose Solutions Based on Trade-offs

I believe there is rarely one solution that is best in every situation. Technical decisions should consider factors such as complexity, performance, maintainability, reliability, and project requirements. I want to understand why a particular approach is appropriate rather than adopting a technology simply because it is popular.

### Build for Maintainability

I value code that is understandable, organized, and easy to change. I aim to separate responsibilities, keep business logic clear, validate inputs, handle errors deliberately, and use abstractions when they make the system easier to maintain. I believe code should be understandable to someone other than the person who originally wrote it.

### Validate Through Testing and Evidence

I believe an implementation should be verified rather than assumed to work. Testing, debugging, reviewing logs, and measuring performance help establish whether a solution behaves as expected. I aim to support claims about correctness and performance with evidence instead of assumptions.

### Learn by Building

I learn technical concepts most effectively by applying them to practical problems. Building projects, encountering unexpected behavior, investigating failures, and improving implementations help me connect theoretical knowledge with real engineering challenges.

### Keep Improving the System

I view software development as an iterative process. An initial implementation may solve the immediate problem, but there is often room to improve its reliability, efficiency, clarity, or usability. I aim to recognize limitations, learn from feedback, and make improvements based on actual needs rather than adding complexity without a clear purpose.
